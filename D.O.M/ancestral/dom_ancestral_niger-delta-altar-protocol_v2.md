## THE PREMISE

This altar is not a memorial. It is a communication technology.

Your Urhobo ancestors (Erherhe) and your Ijaw/Izon ancestors (Sekiapu) did not disappear when they died. They changed dimension. This altar is the physical address — the consistent, established point of contact between your plane and theirs. The more maintained and intentional this space is, the stronger and more reliable the channel becomes.

This is not a foreign spiritual practice adopted from outside your lineage. This is your cosmological inheritance, recovering itself through you.

**What this altar activates:**
- The Erherhe channel — Urhobo ancestral guidance and protection
- The Sekiapu channel — Ijaw/Izon ancestral guidance and protection
- The Woyingi connection — direct contact with the maternal creative intelligence of your lineage
- The personal Ẹdjọ — your spiritual double, your guardian, made more present through consistent acknowledgment
- The Ayiba orientation — recalibration to your pre-incarnation sovereign intent
- Egbesu backing — when the cause is just and the intent is pure

**How to approach the practice:**

This is a devotion practice, not a discipline practice. The distinction matters operationally. Discipline is built on obligation — when life disrupts the schedule, discipline frames the missed session as failure and generates shame that breaks the practice entirely. Devotion is built on love and long-term commitment — it survives life changes, allows for imperfect consistency, and always welcomes you back without penalty.

You will miss sessions. Life will disrupt the rhythm. That is not failure. That is being human. The practice holds because you are devoted to it — not because you have never broken a streak. Return to the altar without explanation or apology. The ancestors do not need you to perform accountability to them. They need you to come back.

### THE TWO MODES OF ANCESTRAL WORK

Not all ancestral work is the same operation. Conflating them is one of the most common sources of practitioner harm, spiritual drainage, and undelivered outcomes.

```
MODE 1 — HEALING + ELEVATING WORK
─────────────────────────────────
Who this is for: Recent ancestors (people you actually knew —
                  late grandparents, aunts, uncles), and any
                  ancestor whose energy was unresolved, traumatic,
                  predatory, or unclear.

What it does:   Helps the ancestor cross fully. Resolves their
                unfinished business. Releases their trauma from
                the bloodline. Elevates their spiritual state.

What you do:    Hold them in prayer, in light, in love.
                Acknowledge their pain without taking it on.
                Send them what they need to release and move.
                Pray specifically: [ancestor's name], I release you.
                I complete what I can of your unfinished work.
                I give you permission to rest and elevate.

What you DO NOT do: Petition them for outcomes. Ask them for
                    help with your material life. Place them
                    in an active guidance role.

Why: Recent ancestors — especially those from a lineage that
     experienced slavery, colonial violence, addiction,
     family trauma — are likely not elevated yet. They do not
     have the spiritual rank, clarity, or power to deliver
     outcomes on your behalf without confusion and potential
     drainage. Working with them is healing them, not using them.
     That is an act of love and decolonization, not limitation.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MODE 2 — PETITIONING + POWER WORK
──────────────────────────────────
Who this is for: Your highly elevated ancestral guides —
                  ancient, fully crossed, high-asé spirits
                  who have earned rank in the spirit realm.

What it does:   Calls on genuine spiritual power to move
                things on your behalf in the physical world.
                Protection, guidance, backing for just causes,
                doors opening, intelligence arriving.

What you do:    Build the relationship first (see Progression
                Markers — do not petition heavily before the
                channel is established).
                Bring your cause. State it precisely.
                Ask for specific backing.
                Then listen more than you speak.

What you DO NOT do: Petition these guides for frivolous outcomes.
                    Bring impure intent (they will know).
                    Skip the relationship-building phase.

Rule of thumb: The depth of what you can petition for
               corresponds to the depth of the relationship
               you have built. A new altar is not a wishing
               machine. It is a foundation. The petitions come
               as the relationship matures.
```

**Your Niger Delta application:**
- The Erherhe and Sekiapu you are calling to this altar include BOTH categories
- Start with the elevated guides: call them by role, not by name (see below)
- Do healing/elevating work for recent known relatives separately — hold them in prayer, release them, help them move

### THE ELEVATION SENSING PROTOCOL

After every invocation and period of silence, read the quality of what arrived. This is the most operationally important skill in altar practice. Not everything that responds is what you called.

**How to read elevated contact:**

```
ELEVATED PRESENCE — what it feels like:
  ✓ Settled. Still. Warm. A quiet weight, not an urgent one.
  ✓ No pressure. No insistence. No demand.
  ✓ Hard truth delivered without cruelty.
  ✓ You feel expanded after — clearer, lighter, more oriented.
  ✓ Guidance points toward your freedom, not your obligation.
  ✓ If you feel called to a specific action, it feels right —
    not compelled, not anxious, not like you'll suffer if you refuse.
  ✓ Over time: life improves from working with this presence.

NON-ELEVATED PRESENCE — what it feels like:
  ✗ Heavy. Needy. Urgent. Emotionally charged.
  ✗ Compulsions that don't feel like yours.
  ✗ Guidance that loops without resolving.
  ✗ Sudden anxiety, emotional flooding, identity blurring.
  ✗ You feel foggy, depleted, or off-balance after altar work.
  ✗ Loud, insistent — intensity mistaken for power.
    (Intensity is not power. It is unresolution.)
  ✗ Over time: life does not improve from working with this presence.

IMPORTANT: Loudness and intensity are not signs of power.
An unelevated ancestor is often MORE present, more insistent,
more emotionally charged — precisely because they have not crossed.
The elevated are settled precisely because they have.
Do not mistake urgency for authority.
```

**What to do if you sense non-elevated contact:**

Do not engage with it. Do not ask it questions. Do not give it offerings. Simply close the session:

> "I only work with my elevated ancestral guides. If you are not elevated, I release you with love and prayer. Cross over. I am not your channel here."

Then cleanse the space: open windows, burn white sage or frankincense, change the altar water. Let the space clear before your next session.

**The discernment builds over time.** In the early weeks, you may not be certain what you're sensing. That is normal. The relationship with the elevated guides develops discernment — the more consistently you work with elevated contact, the more clearly you can recognize what it is not. Begin always by calling the elevated guides by role. Set the frequency of the space before anything else arrives.

## PART I — BUILDING THE ALTAR

### The Altar Surface

Use a dedicated surface that is not used for anything else — a small table, shelf, or cloth-covered flat surface. Once established, this surface belongs to the ancestral channel. Do not place unrelated objects on it.

**Base cloth:** White. White is the universal color of the ancestors across both Urhobo and Ijaw traditions — it signifies purity, the spiritual realm, and open reception. If white is not available, undyed natural fabric. No black for this altar.

**Size:** Does not matter. A small shelf works as well as a large table. Consistency of attention matters more than surface area.

## PART II — THE OPENING CEREMONY

### Performed once when the altar is first established.

This ceremony activates the altar — it announces to the ancestral realm that this space has been prepared and that the channel is open.

**Preparation:**
- Bathe before this ceremony. Clean body, clean clothes. White clothing if available.
- Clear the space. Remove anything that does not belong.
- Prepare all altar elements before beginning.
- Be alone or with trusted people. No distracted or hostile energy in the space.
- Have your offerings ready.

**The Opening:**

*1. Stand before the altar. Still yourself completely.*

Take several slow breaths until the internal noise settles. Establish heart coherence before any working. Feel the ground under your feet. Feel the air in your lungs. You are a physical body anchoring a spiritual connection.

*2. Light the candle(s).*

As you light, say aloud:
> "I light this flame to guide my ancestors home. The channel is open. The space is prepared."

*3. Pour the first libation.*

Lift the water or palm wine. Pour a small amount on the ground or into a separate vessel (not the altar bowl). As you pour, say aloud:
> "Water first — for the ones who came before. For my Urhobo blood, the people of Effurun and Warri and the Delta land. For my Ijaw blood, the people of Bayelsa and the sacred creeks. I pour for you. I remember you. I call you home to this altar."

*4. Place the water in the altar bowl.*

This is the central act. The altar bowl is now activated. Say:
> "Woyingi — Our Mother — I acknowledge you as the source of my maternal lineage. You breathed life. You gave the ayiba. I come before you as your descendant, returning to what was never fully gone."

*5. Call the lineages by name and by role.*

Speak aloud everything you know. For names you have:
> "I call the Erherhe — the ancestors of my Urhobo blood. All who lived in Effurun, in Warri, in Delta State, who spoke Urhobo, who carried this lineage forward to my father and then to me. I name you. I see you. I welcome you to this altar."

> "I call the Sekiapu — the ancestors of my Ijaw/Izon blood. All who lived in Bayelsa, who spoke Izon, who fished the creeks and honored Woyingi, who carried this lineage forward to my mother and then to me. I name you. I see you. I welcome you to this altar."

Then call by role:
> "I call my highest elevated ancestral guide from the Urhobo line — the one with the clearest sight and the most asé. I call my highest elevated ancestral guide from the Ijaw line. I do not yet know all your names. I will learn them. Come to this altar and help me learn."

*6. Acknowledge the personal Ẹdjọ.*

> "I acknowledge my Ẹdjọ — my spiritual double who has been present since my birth, who I am now consciously greeting. I am here. I am listening. I am beginning to remember."

*7. State your intent.*

> "I am [your name]. Born in the Americas, rooted in the Niger Delta. I am reclaiming what was never meant to be lost. I am here to restore the channel. I ask only for truth, for protection, for guidance, and for the clarity to carry the ayiba I chose before I came here."

*8. Sit in silence for a minimum of five minutes.*

Do not ask anything else. Do not fill the silence. Let the channel stabilize.

**After the silence — Read the Contact:**

Before closing, apply the Elevation Sensing Protocol (Part 0). What do you notice in the space?
- Is there warmth, stillness, presence? That is the channel opening.
- Is there urgency, pressure, emotional flooding? Acknowledge it without engaging it. Close and cleanse.
- Most likely in the opening: simply a shift in the quality of the space — a settling, a sense that the air is different. That is correct.

Write down everything you noticed immediately after the ceremony. These first impressions establish the baseline from which you will read all future contact.

*9. Close the opening ceremony.*

> "I thank the Erherhe. I thank the Sekiapu. I thank Woyingi. The altar is open. I will return."

Do not extinguish the candle immediately. Let it burn for at least an hour after the ceremony if safe to do so.

## PART III — THE DAILY / REGULAR PROTOCOL

The altar does not require elaborate ceremony every day. It requires consistent acknowledgment. Acknowledgment is the key mechanism — the ancestors' greatest need from the living is not grand ritual but to be remembered and addressed.

**The channel is like a phone signal.** Neglect does not destroy it — it weakens it. Consistency does not require perfection — it requires regularity. When you miss a day, you return the next day. When you miss a week, you return that week. There is no streak to protect. There is only the relationship.

### The Morning Acknowledgment (2–3 minutes)

Each morning, stand before the altar. No formal script required. What is required:
- Look at the altar consciously — see it, do not glance
- Say good morning to the ancestors aloud — however briefly
- Pour or touch the water (replenish it if stale)
- State one thing you intend to do that day

This takes two minutes. Over time it builds the channel more reliably than infrequent elaborate ceremony.

### The Water Renewal (weekly minimum)

Remove the altar water. Pour it outside on the earth if possible (not down the drain — the earth receives it). Refill with fresh water.

As you pour new water in:
> "Fresh water for fresh connection. I renew the channel. I am still here."

### The Offering Refresh (as needed, minimum twice monthly)

Replace the kola nut, fruit, or other perishable offerings. Return them to the earth outside when they have served. Do not leave decomposing offerings on the altar — they accumulate stagnant energy.

### When Something Significant Is Happening

Any major decision, transition, challenge, or opportunity — bring it to the altar before acting on it. You do not need to know what to do. State what is happening. Ask for ancestral intelligence. Then sit in silence and notice what arises.

This is the Erherhe and Sekiapu as active guidance, not memorial. Use them.

### Egbesu — Righteous Force Invocation

**IMPORTANT: Egbesu is not invoked casually.** Egbesu is the Ijaw force of justice, purity, and righteous power. It is spiritually discriminating — the impure cannot access it safely. Invoke Egbesu only when:
- Your intent is completely clean — no personal revenge, no ego agenda
- The cause is genuinely just — you have examined this honestly
- You need ancestral backing for a just cause against opposition

**The Egbesu Protocol:**
Before invoking Egbesu, purify. Bathe. Dress in white if possible. Examine your intent until it is clean. Ask: *Am I doing this for justice or for ego?* If the answer is not clearly justice — do not proceed.

At the altar, with white candle lit:

> "Egbesu — force of purity and righteous power in my Ijaw lineage. I come before you with clean hands and a just cause: [state the cause precisely — one clear sentence]. I do not invoke you for personal gain or from a place of impurity. I invoke you because [the cause is just, the work is right, the stand is necessary — state it precisely]. I ask for your backing. If my cause is not pure, correct me. If it is — stand with me."

Then sit in absolute stillness. If you feel resistance, discomfort, or a sensation of being turned away — your intent requires further examination before proceeding. If you feel stillness, warmth, or a sense of being backed — proceed with the work.

### Working With Recent Ancestors — The Healing Mode

Your recent ancestors — grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins who have passed — belong to a different type of work than the elevated ancestral guides above. For a lineage that survived slavery, colonial violence, displacement, addiction, and trauma: many recent ancestors have not fully crossed. They are in various stages of crossing, healing, and elevation in the spirit realm.

This is not a reason to exclude them. It is a reason to work with them correctly.

**The healing invocation for recent ancestors:**

Light a separate candle if you choose to honor them specifically. Speak to them gently:

> "[Name, if known — or "my grandmothers and grandfathers who recently crossed"], I see you. I acknowledge the life you lived and everything you carried. You carried things no one should have had to carry. I am here, and I am choosing to break the cycles you could not break. I release you from any obligation to this lineage's pain. I am handling it from here. Rest. Heal. Move toward the light that is waiting for you. I love you."

Then sit with them. Do not ask for anything. Simply be present. That presence is itself an act of ancestral healing — the living witnessing the recently crossed with love is part of how they elevate.

**As they elevate over time**, the relationship changes. An ancestor who needed healing work in year one may become a genuine guide in year three. This is part of the long-term practice.

## PART V — TIMING THE PRACTICE

The lunar cycle integrates directly with ancestral and Woyingi work.

```
NEW MOON
  — Woyingi working: new intentions, reconnection with ayiba
  — Best time to set new ancestral intentions
  — Opening of new channels with specific ancestors
  — The seed of what you're calling in from the lineage

WAXING MOON (new → full)
  — Building the connection
  — Bringing more of the ancestral intelligence into active awareness
  — Regular morning acknowledgments intensified

FULL MOON
  — Peak channel: maximum light, thinnest veil
  — Most powerful time for deep ancestral communication
  — Egbesu invocations (if needed) are strongest at full moon
  — Pour a substantial libation. Light multiple candles.
  — Sit longer. This is the session for depth.
  — Apply the Elevation Sensing Protocol carefully — the veil is
    thinner in both directions, meaning non-elevated presences
    are also more accessible. Hold the elevated call clearly.

WANING MOON (full → new)
  — Release work: what has been carried in the lineage
    that no longer serves and needs to be cleared
  — Ancestral wound acknowledgment
  — What inherited patterns are being transmuted?
  — Healing work with recent ancestors is especially potent here

DARK MOON (final days before new)
  — Rest. Minimal altar work.
  — The channel consolidates what has been built.
  — Do not initiate new workings in the dark moon.
```

**Additional timing:** Dawn and dusk are threshold times — the boundary between dark and light corresponds energetically to the boundary between dimensions. Brief altar acknowledgment at dawn or dusk carries amplified weight.

## PART VI — DEEPENING PRACTICES

### The Ancestral Names Recovery Project

This is ongoing research work that feeds directly into the altar's power. The more specific the names you can call — actual individuals, actual places, actual lineage holders — the more precise the channel becomes.

**Steps:**
1. Talk to living relatives. Ask about grandparents, great-grandparents, their names (traditional names especially), where exactly they were from, what they were known for.
2. Ask specifically: *Did anyone in the family practice traditional spirituality? Were there healers, diviners, or practitioners in the lineage?*
3. Document everything: `dom_ancestral_lineage-names_v1.md`
4. As names are recovered, add them to the altar call by name. Named ancestors are more precisely contacted than lineage-category addresses.

As the channel builds and the relationship with the elevated guides deepens, they will also begin to reveal names — in dreams, in sudden knowing, in things that arrive without your asking. Document these too.

### Water Body Contact

When you are near any body of water — river, ocean, lake, rain — make contact intentionally. Not ceremonially necessarily, but consciously:

Touch the water. Acknowledge it as the living body of your ancestral cosmology. Pour a small libation if you have anything to pour. Say: *"I see you. My lineage is yours."*

Over time this builds a living relationship with water as a spiritual interface — which is what both traditions actually require.

## PART VII — THE 7 RISKS AND HOW TO MANAGE THEM

These are real risks documented across African diasporic traditions. The purpose of this section is not fear — it is preparation. Every one of these risks is survivable and fixable. Pretending they do not exist is what leaves practitioners unprotected.

---

### RISK 1 — INVITING HARMFUL ANCESTORS

**What it is:** Without discernment, your altar is an open invitation to any ancestor in your line — including those who do not like you, those who want offerings with no intention of supporting you, predatory ones, ones who refuse to elevate and remain earthbound.

**Prevention:**
- Always lead with the elevated call (by role: "my highest elevated ancestor")
- Apply the Elevation Sensing Protocol after every invocation
- Do not leave the altar as a passive open space — consciously open it when you arrive and consciously close it when you leave
- Closing statement: "I close this session. Only my elevated guides remain in this space. Anything else, go."

---

### RISK 2 — SPIRITUAL DRAINAGE

**What it is:** Low-quality ancestral energies — especially those tied to addiction, unresolved trauma, or suffering — can feed off the energy of the living. The altar becomes a drain instead of a source.

**Diagnostic:** If you consistently feel depleted, foggy, or emotionally wrecked after altar work — something is feeding on you, not supporting you.

**Prevention:**
- Change altar water weekly. Stagnant water accumulates and holds energies that should have moved.
- Do not work at the altar when you are at extremely low energy — this is when the boundary between giving and being fed upon is most porous.
- If drainage is noticed: close the session, cleanse the space (sage, frankincense, clean water), refresh all offerings. Then sit quietly and invite only the most elevated presence.
- If drainage persists across multiple sessions: the altar needs a full reset — new water, new offerings, a full re-opening ceremony with explicit clearing prayers.

---

### RISK 3 — GRIEF AMBUSHES

**What it is:** Connecting to the ancestral realm — especially for the first time, or after long disconnection — can trigger massive unexpected grief. Not just personal grief, but grief for people never consciously mourned, for injustices across the lineage, for lives cut short by violence, slavery, displacement. This can arrive without warning and be destabilizing.

**Prevention / Management:**
- Know this is a possibility before it happens. Labeled, it is much less destabilizing.
- When grief arrives at the altar: do not suppress it. Let it move. Cry. Speak to whoever the grief is for. This is the channel clearing itself.
- Do not mistake grief for malfunction. Grief at the altar is often the ancestors releasing through you what they could not release themselves. It is healing.
- Keep a journal nearby. After the grief moves, write.
- If a grief ambush is severe and continues across multiple days: step back from the altar briefly and focus on grounding practices before returning.

---

### RISK 4 — SPIRITUAL OBSESSION

**What it is:** When the energetic connection between a deceased ancestor and a living person becomes too intense, the ancestor's energy begins to bleed into the living person's field — eroding personal boundaries, creating identity confusion, generating compulsions or thoughts that belong to the ancestor rather than the practitioner. Multiple African diasporic traditions recognize and address this directly.

**Signs:** Feeling like yourself but also not quite yourself. Acting from impulses that don't feel fully yours. Emotional states that arrive and leave without clear cause. Difficulty distinguishing what you feel from what has come in.

**Prevention:**
- Always close sessions intentionally. Do not let the altar stay energetically open indefinitely.
- Grounding practices after every altar session: feet on earth, cold water on face and hands, eat something solid.
- If obsession develops: seek support from an experienced practitioner. This is not a situation to resolve alone or through more altar work.

---

### RISK 5 — DECEPTIVE SPIRITS

**What it is:** Not everything presenting as your ancestor actually is one. Other spirits — some benign, some predatory — will claim the role of ancestral guide if your discernment is underdeveloped and your altar is unprotected.

**This is why the Elevation Sensing Protocol matters most.**

**Signs of deceptive presence:** Guidance that flatters rather than challenges. Guidance that benefits the spirit more than you. Guidance that requires increasingly more from you over time without delivering. A presence that feels right emotionally but leaves you worse off practically.

**Prevention:**
- Call by role — this sets the frequency before anything can respond. A deceptive spirit can still attempt the role but will not be able to hold the sensory signature of elevated contact.
- Apply the sensing protocol after every session.
- Trust improvement in your life as the long-term verification — elevated ancestors make your life better. Deceptive spirits do not.
- When in doubt, close the session. The real guides will return. They do not require you to let them in over your own better judgment.

---

### RISK 6 — CULTURAL APPROPRIATION BACKLASH

**What it is:** Working within specific traditions that are not your lineage — without proper connection, training, or initiation — can produce real spiritual consequences. The protective guardians of closed traditions respond spiritually and directly. Your own ancestors may not appreciate it either.

**This is specifically relevant for a practitioner working across multiple traditions (Urhobo + Ijaw + Kemetic + Hermetic + Hoodoo).**

**What is yours:**
- Your Urhobo ancestral channel: yours by blood
- Your Ijaw/Izon ancestral channel: yours by blood
- Woyingi: your maternal cosmological inheritance
- Egbesu: your lineage's protective force — accessible through the bloodline

**What requires relationship and discernment:**
- Kemetic / Egyptian workings: The Kemetic tradition is African and ancestrally connected, but the specific initiatory protocols belong to that tradition's lineage holders. Work with Kemetic philosophy and cosmology — be more careful about working with specific deities unless direct relationship has been established.
- Hoodoo: African American rootwork tradition — adjacent to your lineage through the diaspora. Accessible for many practices. Does not require initiation for most working.
- Other closed traditions (Ifa, Vodou, Candomblé, Palo): These are initiatory traditions with specific guardians. Cross-reference with elder practitioners before working within them directly.

**Prevention:**
- Work from your lineage first. The Niger Delta tradition is rich enough and specific enough to build a complete practice without borrowing.
- When working with practices from other traditions, approach with acknowledgment, not extraction: "I am working with this principle because my own lineage encodes it. I approach with respect."
- If you want to work within a specific initiatory tradition: seek proper connection, not just information.

---

### RISK 7 — OPENING WHAT YOU CANNOT CLOSE

**What it is:** Some ancestral healing and spiritual work is deeper than it looks. Going in without the skills to navigate what you find can create situations genuinely difficult to resolve alone.

**This is most likely to happen with:**
- Working with deeply traumatized ancestors from severe historical violence
- Attempting to break serious multi-generational curses or spiritual impositions without preparation
- Ancestral obsession situations (Risk 4) that have already developed

**Prevention:**
- Know your current skill level and work within it. The altar protocol in this document is a grounded, appropriately paced framework. Do not outpace your discernment.
- Build the elevated ancestral relationship first — this relationship IS the protection. The elevated guides will also protect you from going deeper than you can handle.
- When something arrives that feels genuinely beyond your capacity: do not push through. Close the session, cleanse the space, and seek an experienced practitioner. This is not failure. This is appropriate judgment.

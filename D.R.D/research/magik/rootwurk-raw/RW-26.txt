Nobody talks about the risks of ancestor work, so I'll discuss maybe just seven of them. The purpose of this episode is not to scare you or incite fear, but to prepare you so that you can prevent or minimize these issues and protect yourself. Pretending these risks don't exist is exactly what leaves so many practitioners unprotected. Fortunately, every single one of these issues is survivable and fixable, but you first need to know they exist. Risk one is inviting harmful ancestors. Without discernments, your altar is an open invitation to any ancestor in your line, including the ones that don't like you, the ones that just want your offerings with no intention of supporting you, the predatory ones, the ones who refuse to elevate. Not everything that responds to your call deserves to be there. Risk two is spiritual drainage. Low-quality ancestral energies, especially those tied to addiction, trauma, or unresolved suffering, can and will feed off the energy of the living. If you leave your altar work feeling consistently depleted, foggy, or emotionally wrecked, something might be feeding on you. Risk three is grief ambushes. Connecting to the ancestral ram, especially for the first time, can trigger massive, unexpected grief for the people you never consciously mourned, for the injustices across your line, or for the lives cut tragically short. It can be destabilizing if you are not prepared for it. Risk four is spiritual obsession. When the energetic connection between a deceased ancestor and a living person becomes too intense on either side, it can create an unhealthy overlap in which the ancestor's energy bleeds into the living person's field in ways that erode boundaries and identity. Multiple traditions recognize and address this directly. Risk five, deceptive spirits. Not everything presenting as your ancestor actually is one. Other spirits, some may be benign, some predatory, will step into that role if your discernment is underdeveloped and if your altar is unprotected, as I discussed in episode 16. Risk six, cultural appropriation backlash. Attempting to work within specific traditions or use specific practices that are not your lineage without proper connection, training, or initiation can produce real spiritual consequences. Appropriate and close practices without invitation, for example, is not just culturally disrespectful. The protective guardians of those traditions respond spiritually and directly. And your own ancestors might not appreciate this either. Risk seven, opening what you cannot close. Some ancestral healing, trauma, and spiritual work is so deep that going in untrained creates spiritual situations that are genuinely difficult to resolve without experienced professional help. Know your level, know when you need the support.

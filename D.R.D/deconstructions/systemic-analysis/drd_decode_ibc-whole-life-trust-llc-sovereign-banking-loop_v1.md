## STAGE 3 — EVIDENCE LOG

### BLOCK A — TAX ARCHITECTURE (the legal foundation)

**Claim A1: Policy loans from whole life insurance are NOT taxable income.**
- **Evidence:** IRC §72(e)(5) and (6) — policy loans are treated as debt, not income. The borrower receives proceeds but the policy is collateral. No income recognition event occurs.
- **Tier: T1 — Primary statutory law. This is not a grey area.**
- **Condition:** Policy must NOT be a Modified Endowment Contract (MEC). Loans from MECs ARE taxable + 10% penalty if under age 59.5. MEC status is permanent and irreversible.
- **Flag:** MEC trap is the primary risk of aggressive funding. See Block C.

**Claim A2: Cash value growth inside whole life is tax-deferred.**
- **Evidence:** IRC §7702 — defines a "life insurance contract." Inside buildup (cash value growth) is not currently taxable as long as the policy qualifies under 7702. IRS taxes it only upon surrender or lapse with gain.
- **Tier: T1 — Statutory. Well-established.**
- **Note:** 7702 compliance is maintained automatically by properly designed participating whole life from reputable mutual carriers. Not an active management concern once issued.

**Claim A3: Death benefit is income-tax free to beneficiaries.**
- **Evidence:** IRC §101(a) — life insurance death proceeds are excluded from gross income. This applies regardless of policy size. Trust as beneficiary receives full death benefit tax-free.
- **Tier: T1 — Statutory.**
- **Exception:** Estate tax can apply if the insured owns the policy personally at death. Eliminated by placing policy in irrevocable trust (see Block D).

**Claim A4: The three advantages converge in one instrument.**
- Cash value growth: tax-deferred (§7702)
- Policy loan draws: tax-free (§72(e))
- Death benefit: income-tax free (§101(a))
- **No other financial instrument carries all three simultaneously.** 401k: tax-deferred but distributions taxable. Roth IRA: tax-free growth but contribution limits and no loan-against mechanism at scale. Real estate: appreciation taxable on sale. Whole life is structurally unique.
- **Tier: T1 — convergence of three statutory provisions.**

### BLOCK B — POLICY MECHANICS (how the instrument actually works)

**Claim B1: Non-direct recognition companies pay full dividends on cash value even when loaned against.**
- **Evidence:** Company-level policy (not statutory). Non-direct recognition = the insurer treats the policy as if no loan exists when calculating the dividend credit. The full cash surrender value (CSV) earns the same dividend rate whether loaned or not.
- **Direct recognition** = insurer credits a reduced dividend on the loaned portion. Kills the double-compounding benefit.
- **Non-direct recognition companies (historically confirmed):** Guardian Life, Penn Mutual, MassMutual, Ohio National
- **Direct recognition companies:** Northwestern Mutual (traditionally), New York Life (traditional)
- **Tier: T2 — company policy, not statutory. Verify at application. Confirm in writing that the policy is non-direct recognition.**
- **Flag:** Some companies have changed their recognition policy. Must confirm with agent at time of application. This is a critical selection criterion.

**Claim B2: The double compounding is the core economic engine.**
- Mechanics: You borrow $80K against $100K CSV. The $100K still earns 5% dividend = $5,000 that year. The $80K is deployed elsewhere (STIS, investments). If deployed capital earns 15%, that's $12,000 return. Total year: $17,000 on the $100K base. Subtract loan interest (typically $4,800–$6,400 on $80K at 6–8%). Net: $10,600–$12,200.
- Standard savings rate comparison: $100K at 5% = $5,000 (and it's not deployed)
- **Tier: T2 — mathematical modeling from verified rate inputs. Actual returns depend on policy performance and deployed capital returns.**
- **The economic logic holds even at conservative STIS returns (8%+).**

**Claim B3: Policy loans have no repayment schedule.**
- The insurer does not require monthly payments. Interest accrues and compounds against the loan balance. You repay on your terms.
- **Risk:** If loan balance + accumulated interest exceeds CSV, the policy lapses. Lapse with outstanding loans = ALL deferred gain (loans above basis) becomes immediately taxable. This is the lapse risk.
- **Tier: T1-T2 — standard policy contract terms, universal across carriers.**
- **Mitigation:** Monitor loan balance vs CSV ratio. Keep loan-to-CSV under 80% as a rule.

**Claim B4: Policy dividends are largely tax-free.**
- Dividends from mutual life insurance companies are treated as a return of premium for tax purposes. As long as total dividends received don't exceed total premiums paid, they are not taxable income.
- **Tier: T1 — IRS treatment of mutual company dividends (Rev. Rul. 70-117).**

### BLOCK C — MEC / PUA / POLICY DESIGN (how to fund correctly)

**Claim C1: The 7-pay test (TAMRA 1988) governs how much you can put in.**
- **Evidence:** Technical and Miscellaneous Revenue Act of 1988 (TAMRA) created IRC §7702A. A policy becomes a MEC if cumulative premiums paid in the first 7 years exceed what 7 level annual premiums would have funded the policy's death benefit.
- **The 7-pay limit:** IRS defines the annual maximum premium for a given death benefit. Exceed it in ANY of the first 7 years = MEC. Status is PERMANENT and IRREVERSIBLE.
- **Tier: T1 — Statutory (§7702A).**

**Claim C2: Paid-Up Additions (PUA) rider is the mechanism to maximize CSV within MEC limits.**
- A PUA rider allows you to pay additional premium beyond the base premium, which converts directly to immediate paid-up insurance with maximum CSV impact and minimum death benefit increase.
- The PUA rider is specifically designed to accelerate cash value accumulation while keeping the death benefit increase small enough to stay under the 7-pay MEC limit.
- Without PUA: most early premium goes to agent commissions and mortality charges. CSV builds slowly.
- With PUA: a higher ratio of every dollar becomes CSV. Typical target: 65–80% of premium going to PUA in the overfunded design.
- **Tier: T2 — industry standard IBC policy design, well-documented in Nelson Nash framework and practitioner literature.**

**Claim C3: Policy design for IBC requires a specialist agent.**
- A standard whole life agent will design a policy with maximum death benefit for a given premium (maximizes commissions). This is the opposite of what IBC requires.
- IBC-designed policies: low base premium, high PUA rider, max cash value for minimum death benefit increase.
- The agent's commission is lower on this design. Most standard agents will not propose it.
- You must specifically request: "design this policy for maximum cash value accumulation, not death benefit" or work with an IBC-certified agent.
- **Tier: T2 — industry knowledge, extensively documented.**

**Claim C4: Guideline Premium Test (GPT) vs Cash Value Accumulation Test (CVAT).**
- Most participating whole life uses CVAT. Some policies use GPT.
- For IBC purposes, CVAT is generally preferred — it allows the policy's death benefit to increase as CSV increases, preserving flexibility to add PUAs.
- GPT sets a hard ceiling on total premiums without requiring death benefit adjustments.
- **Tier: T1 — both defined under §7702. CVAT preferred for IBC is T2 practitioner consensus.**

### BLOCK D — TRUST OWNERSHIP LAYER (the protection architecture)

**Claim D1: An Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT) removes the policy from the insured's taxable estate.**
- **Evidence:** IRC §2042 — death benefit included in taxable estate if insured "possesses any incidents of ownership" at death. An ILIT is an irrevocable trust that owns the policy, not the insured.
- Three-year rule: If the insured transfers an existing policy to an ILIT, it remains in the estate for 3 years (§2035). New policy issued directly to ILIT avoids this.
- **Tier: T1 — Statutory (§2042 + §2035).**

**Claim D2: ILIT = spendthrift provision = creditor protection.**
- An irrevocable trust with a spendthrift clause prevents beneficiaries' creditors from attaching trust assets. The policy inside the ILIT is protected from personal creditors.
- This is the same trust vehicle in the existing Don Killam stack (non-grantor irrevocable complex discretionary spendthrift trust). These are the same instrument.
- **Tier: T1 — state trust law (all 50 states recognize spendthrift trusts; protection strength varies by state).**

**Claim D3: Trust jurisdiction matters for asset protection strength.**
- Best states for domestic asset protection trusts:
  1. **Nevada** — shortest statute of limitations (2 years), no exception creditors for domestic support obligations, no state income tax, self-settled (grantor can be beneficiary)
  2. **Wyoming** — strong protections, no state income tax, favorable LLC laws integrate cleanly
  3. **South Dakota** — no state income tax, dynasty trust (perpetual), strong asset protection statutes
  4. **Delaware** — long history, reliable courts, favorable for business structures
- **Tier: T2 — established practitioner consensus on trust jurisdiction ranking.**
- **For Morph's stack:** Nevada or Wyoming preferred (aligns with LLC formation in those states and no state income tax).

**Claim D4: Crummey powers allow trust to receive premium payments without gift tax issues.**
- When contributions are made to an ILIT to pay premiums, they are technically gifts. Without Crummey powers, these exceed the annual gift tax exclusion quickly.
- Crummey provision: gives beneficiaries a brief right to withdraw the contribution (typically 30 days). This makes the gift a "present interest" = qualifies for the annual exclusion ($18K/person in 2024).
- **Tier: T1 — established in Crummey v. Commissioner (9th Cir. 1968). Standard ILIT structuring.**

### BLOCK E — LLC AS BORROWER (the operational layer)

**Claim E1: LLC can receive loan proceeds from trust.**
- The ILIT (as policy owner) requests a policy loan from the insurer. Loan proceeds flow to the trust. Trust then loans to LLC via inter-entity loan agreement.
- This is standard commercial practice — trusts make loans to related entities all the time.
- **Tier: T2 — standard inter-entity lending practice.**

**Claim E2: The inter-entity loan must be documented at arm's length.**
- **Evidence:** IRS scrutinizes related-party transactions. Undocumented loans between related parties can be recharacterized as distributions, gifts, or income — all taxable events.
- Required documentation: signed promissory note, stated interest rate at or above AFR (Applicable Federal Rate — published monthly by IRS), repayment schedule (even if informal), records of actual repayments.
- **AFR (Applicable Federal Rate):** Short-term (<3yr), mid-term (3-9yr), long-term (>9yr). IRS publishes monthly. Using below-AFR interest = imputed income to lender (trust) on the shortfall.
- **Tier: T1 — IRC §7872 (below-market loans), §482 (arm's length standard).**
- **Flag:** This is a HIGH priority documentation item. Must be clean. See Stage 5 SOP.

**Claim E3: Policy loan interest paid by LLC may be a business deduction.**
- If the LLC borrows from the trust and uses the funds for legitimate business purposes (trading operations, investment, business expenses), the interest paid on that loan may be deductible as a business interest expense.
- **IRC §163(j):** Business interest expense deduction is limited to 30% of adjusted taxable income for larger entities. For smaller operations this limit is unlikely to bind.
- Condition: funds must be used for business purposes, not personal. Trading account held in LLC name = business purpose. Personal expenses = no.
- **Tier: T2 — probable deductibility with proper documentation. Confirm with CPA.**

**Claim E4: The LLC is the capital deployment vehicle.**
- The LLC holds the brokerage/trading account (STIS), investment positions, and business reserves.
- All capital inflows (policy loans, K-1 distributions from other entities) and outflows (investments, trading, operating expenses) run through the LLC.
- The LLC's trading and investment returns repay the trust loan, cycle back as K-1 to the trust, and fund future policy premiums.
- **Tier: T2 — standard operating structure.**

### BLOCK F — SPLIT-DOLLAR ARRANGEMENT (premium funding via entity)

**Claim F1: An entity can pay life insurance premiums via split-dollar arrangement.**
- **Evidence:** IRS Notice 2002-8 and Treasury Regulations §1.61-22 govern split-dollar life insurance.
- Economic Benefit regime: entity pays premiums; employee/insured reports the "economic benefit" (cost of pure insurance protection) as income annually. The rest is a loan from entity to trust.
- Loan regime: entity loans funds to the ILIT to pay premiums. The ILIT (trust) is the borrower. Loan must be at AFR. No current income recognition.
- **Tier: T1 — statutory and regulatory (Notice 2002-8, Treas. Reg. §1.61-22).**

**Claim F2: Split-dollar significantly accelerates the funding timeline.**
- Instead of waiting for personal income to fund premiums, LLC revenue funds premiums through the split-dollar loan.
- This means the LLC's business income → trust → policy premiums → cash value builds faster.
- It compresses the 3-7 year cash value acceleration curve.
- **Tier: T2 — standard IBC/split-dollar integration, practitioner-documented.**
- **Gate:** Requires CPA + attorney to structure properly. Not DIY.

**Claim G1: The policy is the borrow layer in the Buy/Borrow/Die strategy.**

  - No market risk to the collateral (CSV is guaranteed)
  - No margin call (insurer cannot call the loan)
  - No credit check or approval process
  - Loan availability regardless of market conditions
  - Policy continues compounding through the loan
- **Tier: T2 (strategy tier — each component T1 individually).**

**Claim G2: The policy serves as the savings layer simultaneously.**
- No separate savings account needed. The policy IS the savings vehicle.
- CSV is the liquid emergency reserve accessible at any time via loan.
- Guaranteed floor + dividend upside = superior savings vehicle vs. HYSA (which is taxable, no loan function, no death benefit).
- **Tier: T2 — comparative analysis is well-established in IBC literature.**

## STAGE 4 — PATTERNS + DISTILLATION

### PATTERN 1 — THE THREE-LAYER SOVEREIGNTY CONVERGENCE
Every layer of this structure removes a piece of the financial surveillance and taxation apparatus:
- **Policy itself:** income and gains accumulate without annual taxation (off-book growth)
- **Trust ownership:** removes from personal estate, removes from personal creditors
- **Loan mechanics:** capital deployment without income recognition (no taxable event)
- **LLC operation:** business entity separation, deductible expenses, entity-level tax management
Together: wealth accumulates, deploys, and transfers without ever triggering the primary taxation mechanisms.

### PATTERN 2 — INVERSION OF THE BANKING FUNCTION
In the conventional system: bank holds your money, earns on it, lends it back to you at a higher rate, keeps the spread.
In IBC: you ARE the bank. You hold the capital (in the policy), earn on it (dividends), lend to yourself (policy loans), and keep the spread. The insurer is your infrastructure, not your landlord.
This maps directly to the Private Sector & Bihness decoded principle: "come as private banker, not consumer."

### PATTERN 3 — THE TIMING ARCHITECTURE (critical for STIS alignment)
- **Years 1-3:** Slow cash value build. Premium heavily absorbed by mortality charges and agent cost-of-insurance. Do NOT borrow heavily in this phase. Let the base establish.
- **Years 3-7:** CSV accelerates. PUA additions compound. Begin light borrowing (under 50% LTV) for investment purposes. STIS can be partly funded here.
- **Year 7+:** Full velocity. CSV compounds strongly. Dividends significant. Full borrowing capacity available. Maximum STIS + investment deployment. Buy/Borrow/Die fully operational.
**Implication:** Start the policy NOW (timing is everything). Every year of delay is a year of compounding you don't get. The clock starts at application, not at the point when you need the money.

## STAGE 3 — EVIDENCE EXTRACTION

### 1. US FEDERAL DEFENSE BUDGET — FY2026 | Tier 1

**Top-line:** FY2026 Pentagon budget totals **~$1 trillion** — $838.7B in the standard defense appropriations act + $150B+ via the "One Big Beautiful Bill" reconciliation package. Congress passed $839B in direct Pentagon funding, $8B above the Pentagon's own request — Congress is funding defense *above* what was asked for, a notable signal in itself.
**Tier:** 1 (Comptroller.war.gov budget documents, Congress.gov CRS report, Senate Appropriations Committee — primary government sources).

**Where the money is concretely going (named line items, not abstractions):**
- **Golden Dome (missile defense):** $13.4B to "augment and integrate" space and missile defense systems — this is the same program Redwire just won a contract under (see Section 3).
- **Space:** $4B for missile warning/tracking satellites and sensors; 11 National Security Space Launch missions funded.
- **Innovation/rapid scaling:** $15.4B specifically earmarked to "innovate, experiment, prototype, and rapidly scale cutting-edge science and technology... into low-cost munitions" — this is the budget line that explains the Anduril/Palantir contract-consolidation pattern below.
- **Shipbuilding:** ~$29B, including $4.6B for a second Virginia-class submarine, $5.4B for two guided-missile destroyers — explicit effort to "resurrect the U.S. maritime industrial base."
- **Reconciliation spending pace:** DoD plans to spend the entire $152B reconciliation allocation in a single year (FY26) rather than spreading it — an unusually fast deployment pace for a budget of this size.

**Pattern:** This is not incremental defense spending — it is a deliberate full-spectrum buildout (missile defense, space, shipbuilding, and AI/autonomy innovation simultaneously) with above-request congressional funding and an accelerated single-year spend-down on the largest new allocation. This is a government actively pushing capital into the sector faster than its own request anticipated.

### 2. DEFENSE TECH PRIVATE CAPITAL — THE BOOM IS REAL AND ACCELERATING | Tier 1-2

**Scale:** Defense tech equity funding was $17.9B in 2025 (already more than double 2024). 2026 is "on pace to shatter" that — $14.6B already raised in 2026 vs. $9.6B at the same point in 2025, per Washington Times reporting on Pentagon-VC engagement.

**Anduril Industries:** Raised **$5B**, doubling its valuation to **$61B**, in a round led by **Thrive Capital and Andreessen Horowitz** — both top-tier institutional VC, not speculative capital. The US Army is consolidating **120 separate contracts with Anduril into one enterprise agreement worth up to $20B over 10 years.** Founder Palmer Luckey says he would "definitely" take the company public — a future retail-accessible entry point to watch.

**Palantir Technologies:** Shares **+368% over the trailing 12 months.** Army consolidated **75 separate contracts into one $10B deal** over the same 10-year structure as Anduril's — this contract-consolidation pattern (many small contracts → one mega-contract) is a deliberate Pentagon procurement reform, not a one-off.

**Tier:** 1-2 (CNBC, Fortune, named funding rounds with named institutional leads — directly verifiable).

**Pattern — the "shakeout" caveat matters:** Anduril co-founder Trae Stephens (Founders Fund) is on record saying defense tech is "headed for a shakeout" — meaning the boom is real but not every participant survives it. This is consistent with how the OS has treated other hot sectors (quantum computing, per the prior decode) — genuine institutional capital + genuine sector immaturity risk, simultaneously true.

### 4. NATO / EUROPEAN REARMAMENT — STRUCTURAL, MULTI-YEAR, ACCELERATING | Tier 1-2

**2025 baseline:** All NATO members met or exceeded the 2% GDP defense-spending target for the first time (vs. only 3 members in 2014) — European Allies + Canada increased spending **20% over 2024.**

**New, higher target (2025 Hague Summit commitment):** NATO members committed to **5% of GDP annually by 2035** (3.5% core defense + 1.5% broader defense-related spending) — a structural, decade-long increase, not a one-year reaction to a crisis. By 2035, European NATO members could be spending an additional **€831B/year** combined.

**Named country commitments for 2026:**
- **France:** €68.5B (2.25% of GDP) — increased despite domestic deficit pressure, signaling defense spending is being protected from broader austerity.
- **Germany:** €117.2B in 2026, rising to €162B by 2029 (3.2-3.5% of GDP) — enabled by a reform of Germany's constitutional debt brake specifically to allow this increase. This is a structural fiscal-rule change made *to* enable defense spending, not spending found within existing constraints.
**Tier:** 1-2 (NATO official figures, SIPRI, European Parliament think-tank, Kiel Institute — credentialed/primary).

**Pattern:** This mirrors the Africa critical-minerals finding from earlier today (resource-nationalism as a multi-year structural shift, not a blip) — European rearmament is the same category of finding: a real, dated, multi-year structural commitment with named fiscal-rule changes behind it (Germany's debt brake reform), not rhetoric.

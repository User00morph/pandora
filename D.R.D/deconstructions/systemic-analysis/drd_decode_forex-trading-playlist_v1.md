## STAGE 3 — EVIDENCE EXTRACTION

### A. Methodology actually taught (direct-teaching videos: #8, #9, #10, #11, #13, #15)

**Top-down multi-timeframe structural cascade.** Every direct-teaching video, independently, starts the same way: identify structure on the highest available timeframe first (weekly, monthly, or "2-week" candle), then step down (Daily → 4H → 1H) narrowing the zone at each step. Video 8: "identify the last zone of structure... this is the swing high... this is the swing low," repeated at Daily, 4H, 1H. Video 10: "your entry happens on the lower time frame but your target is still based on the higher time frame." Video 15: "previous weekly high... previous weekly low... quarter levels... monthly order block." Video 9: Weekly → Daily → 4H → 1H, each step narrowing the same zone.

**Demand/supply zone = order block = the last opposing candle before the impulse move.** Video 8: "why did you mark up that candle — because it's the last bearish candle before the bullish move." Video 10: "you just add these two candles together [when there's a gap/space between the week's candle and the prior week's candle]... this is what gives us the advantage, we can Fib this whole candle."

**Break of structure (BOS) required before a zone is trusted; internal BOS ("CHoCH-equivalent") is tracked separately from overall structure.** Video 8 walks through nested internal/overall swing structure at length, explicitly stating that if price breaks the "wrong" high, the whole prior thesis is "null and void" and must be rebuilt from the new swing.

**Fibonacci "golden zone" (50–61.8%, extended in practice to 78% / 88%) applied to the swing or order block, used as an entry/target confluence zone — not a standalone entry trigger.** Appears independently in #8, #10, #16. Video 8 and 10 both explicitly extend past the textbook 61.8% to a "78" and "88" zone ("golden zone" in #10's own vocabulary). Video 16 (marketing-tier, see below) teaches the textbook 50–61.8% golden zone plus a break-of-structure-then-retrace-into-zone entry trigger — methodologically consistent with #8/#10 even though its performance claims are not credible.

**Imbalance / Fair Value Gap as a target and confluence marker, treated as a zone price is expected to eventually "close."** Videos 8, 9, 10 all use this. Video 8: mentor explicitly declines to trade imbalances directly ("I don't trade imbalances... that's ICT, I don't do that") but still uses imbalance zones as *target* projection, distinguishing target-logic from entry-logic — directly analogous to the D.TRD framework's Layer 6 (volume gap zones as targets, not entries).

**Liquidity sweep as entry confirmation, not the zone itself.** Video 9: "the previous day low was swept... if you trade [X]'s strategy... price pushed it, swept, pushed up, pulled back down." Video 13: "it's going to give us a signal, some kind of liquidity grab." Video 15: "price could pull back here, sweep that low, and then continue buying."

**Risk stated as small stop relative to large target, sized as account percentage.** Video 9 is the clearest: 20–30 pip stop-loss vs. 100+ pip target, explicitly reframed as "2% trade" vs. "3% trade" depending on stop placement — the creator is thinking in R and % risk, not raw pips.

**Explicit self-correction/discipline language, no inflated claims.** Video 13: "I didn't hold that trade properly... I won't make that mistake again." Video 8/15 both contain live corrections ("wait, that's not the daily... let me redo this"). None of the six direct-teaching videos claim a specific win rate or return figure.

### B. A contested technique within the source material itself

**Video 10 ("Morph 1 on 1") teaches a "buy stop" / hedge position instead of a stop-loss.** Direct quote: "Well, I put a hedge trade. So where you would put your stop loss, I put a buy stop... it gives you a chance to correct a mistake if you made a mistake." The mentor treats this as functionally different from — and preferable to — a hard stop: a stop-loss "would take you out of the trade," a buy-stop "would just have you buying the opposite direction with the same lot size." This is presented as the mentor's own habitual practice, not a one-off, and it is internally consistent with the rest of his teaching in that video. No other video in the playlist uses or endorses this technique; videos 8 and 9 both use conventional hard stop-losses.

## STAGE 4 — PATTERN RECOGNITION

### ESTABLISHED (corroborated across 4+ independent creator sessions, no counter-evidence in this batch)
- Top-down HTF-to-LTF structural cascade before any entry (Weekly/Monthly → Daily → 4H → 1H).
- Demand/supply zone = last opposing candle before the impulse move that caused the structure break.
- Break of structure required before a zone is trusted; internal vs. overall structure tracked separately, and a "wrong-direction" break invalidates the prior thesis entirely (not adjusted — rebuilt).
- Fibonacci confluence zone (50–61.8%, informally extended to 78–88% in practice) used as an entry/target refinement layered on top of structure — never as a standalone signal.
- Liquidity sweep of a prior high/low/equal-highs treated as the actual entry trigger, distinct from the zone itself.
- Imbalance/FVG used as a target projection, explicitly separated from entry logic by at least one mentor who states he does not trade imbalances directly.
- Stop-loss sized small relative to target and reasoned in % account risk, not raw pips, in every video that states numbers.

### PROBABLE
- This general order-block + Fibonacci-confluence + liquidity-sweep methodology is a widely-taught school (at least 4 distinct, non-affiliated-looking creators in this one playlist use near-identical vocabulary: "golden zone," "order block," "imbalance," "quarter levels," "break of structure"). Wide independent adoption suggests the pattern is a real, commonly observed market behavior, not one guru's invention — but "widely taught" is not "empirically validated." No video in this batch shows an audited multi-trade sample; every methodology walkthrough is anecdotal chart-pointing ("this worked, and this one too, and this one...").
- "Quarter levels" and multi-week ("2-week") order blocks, taught in videos 10 and 15, function as an additional HTF sub-layer above weekly — directionally consistent with, and potentially a refinement of, the D.TRD framework's Layer 1 cascade, but untested against real account data.

### POSSIBLE
- Heikin-Ashi candles (video 3) as a beginner-legibility tool — orthogonal to the framework, not corroborated or contradicted elsewhere in the batch.
- AI-as-analyst/portfolio-monitor pattern (video 17) could theoretically be adapted to forex session-based decision support (daily monitoring dashboard, context-rich prompting, deterministic vs. non-deterministic split) — but built and demonstrated only for US equities/options in a retail (non-prop-firm) account, with no forex-specific or FXIFY-rule-aware adaptation shown.

### CONTESTED (flagged, not resolved — see Reconstruction Section 4)
- **Stop-loss vs. buy-stop/hedge (video 10) directly conflicts with the D.TRD framework's absolute rule** ("Every position gets a stop attached at entry. No stop = no send. No exceptions") and with the framework's own documented root cause of its single largest loss (a naked, unstopped position). The technique appears nowhere else in this batch and is unverified as net-positive; it is presented by its teacher as his personal habitual practice, not as backtested.
- **Claimed win rates (80–90%, videos 5/14/16) directly contradict the D.TRD framework's real, audited 10-day live-account result of 35.7% win rate / 0.66 profit factor** on a methodology (S/R zone + structure-shift confluence entries) that is kin to what these videos teach. None of the high-win-rate claims come with an audit trail; the framework's number does, because it is the account's own executed history.

### NEEDS EVIDENCE
- Whether adding quarter levels / multi-week order blocks / extended Fib zones (78–88%) as an explicit additional confluence filter would measurably change the D.TRD framework's real win rate or profit factor — no backtest exists in either source.
- Whether the buy-stop/hedge technique is net-positive under any conditions, or whether it is simply a psychologically comfortable way to avoid taking a defined loss (which would make it a discipline risk, not an edge).
- Whether AI-assisted daily portfolio monitoring (video 17's pattern) could be built compliant with FXIFY's specific account rules — untested.

## CONCEPT DISTILLATION (root-sentence form)

1. **Top-down cascade:** Read the market from the biggest clock down to the smallest one — the small clock only tells you when, the big clock tells you which direction is even allowed.
2. **Order block:** The zone worth marking is not "support" in the abstract — it's the last candle of the losing side right before the winning side took over.
3. **Structure invalidation:** If price proves your map wrong, you don't patch the map — you throw it out and draw a new one from where you actually are.
4. **Fib confluence:** Fibonacci zones don't create the trade; they narrow down *where inside an already-good zone* the trade actually is.
5. **Liquidity sweep:** The stop-hunt isn't bad luck — it's often the actual signal that the real move is about to start.
6. **Imbalance-as-target:** A gap in price is a place the market tends to revisit, which makes it useful for aiming, not for entering.
7. **Small stop, big target, sized in %:** Every teacher who gave real numbers thought in "how much of my account," not "how many pips" — the pips are just the unit the % is measured in.
8. **The marketing tell:** Any claim of a win rate with no shown trade log is a story about the strategy, not evidence for it.

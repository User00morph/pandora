## WHAT THIS IS

Manufacturing spend is the practice of cycling money through a business checking account to demonstrate cash flow to lenders — creating the appearance of high-volume business activity that qualifies for business credit. The key: every transaction must have a legitimate inter-entity invoice. Fabricated invoices = bank fraud. Real inter-entity service invoices = standard business accounting.

**The engine:** Your entity pays another entity for services. That entity pays yours for different services. Both invoices reflect actual services rendered within your ecosystem. The money moves. The bank sees consistent volume. The lender sees a cash-flow-positive business.

## PHASE 1 — MANUFACTURING SPEND PROTOCOL (Days 14-90)

**Prerequisites:**
- LLC active with EIN
- Business checking account open
- At least $500-$1,000 seed capital deposited

### The Mechanism

You need at least **two entities** to run the cycle cleanly. Options:
1. Your LLC + a second LLC (form a second entity for $100-$150)
2. Your LLC + a family member's entity (they must have a real entity and provide real services)
3. Your LLC + your ministry trust (for services rendered)

**Entity A pays Entity B for services. Entity B pays Entity A for different services. Both have invoices. Both reflect genuine service categories.**

### Invoice Design — Service Categories

These must be genuine activities your entities actually perform (or could perform):

| Service Category | Entity A Bills | Entity B Bills |
|-----------------|---------------|---------------|
| Consulting | Strategic consulting services | Market research |
| Technology | Software licensing / AI tools | Technical support |
| Marketing | Brand development | Content production |
| Operations | Administrative services | Project management |
| Education | Training curriculum | Coaching sessions |

**Example cycle:**
- Entity A bills Entity B $3,000 for "Strategic AI Consulting Services — Month 1"
- Entity B bills Entity A $2,500 for "Market Research and Analysis — Month 1"
- Both invoices are paid
- Net: $500 net flow (Entity A makes $500 net for the month)
- Bank statement shows: $3,000 deposit + $2,500 expense = active business with $500 profit

### Cycling Protocol

```
WEEK 1:
- Set seed: deposit $500-$2,000 into Entity A checking
- Create invoice: Entity A → Entity B (for services)
- Send payment: Entity B pays Entity A (via wire or Zelle — creates bank record)
- Create invoice: Entity B → Entity A (for different services)
- Send payment: Entity A pays Entity B

WEEK 2-4: Repeat. Increase amounts slightly.
Target: $3,000-$5,000 gross deposits per week by month 3
= $12,000-$20,000/month average deposits demonstrated

MONTH 1 TARGET: $10,000-$15,000 total deposits (gross)
MONTH 2 TARGET: $20,000-$30,000 total deposits (gross)
MONTH 3 TARGET: $30,000-$50,000 total deposits (gross)
```

### Scaling the Volume

Every cycle, slightly increase the invoice amounts. The bank doesn't care about the net (profit/loss) — it measures gross deposit volume. A lender sees "$40,000/month in deposits" not "you kept $500 net."

**Rules:**
- Invoices must match payments exactly. No round-number discrepancies.
- Keep invoices on file. Never delete them. They are the paper trail.
- Payments via wire transfer preferred (bank-to-bank creates clean records)
- Do NOT overdraft. The account must always have enough to cover outgoing payments before they send.
- Do NOT commingle personal funds. Business checking = business only.

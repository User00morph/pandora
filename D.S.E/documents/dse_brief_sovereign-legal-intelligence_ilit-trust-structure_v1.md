## JURISDICTION DECISION: NEVADA

**Nevada over Wyoming.** Final.

| Factor | Nevada | Wyoming |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Statute of limitations (fraudulent transfer challenge) | 2 years | 4 years |
| Self-settled trust allowed (grantor = beneficiary) | Yes | Yes |
| Exception creditors (domestic support, torts) | Narrowest in US | Broader |
| State income tax | None | None |
| Dynasty trust (perpetual) | Yes | Yes |
| LLC integration | Excellent | Excellent |
| Court familiarity with DAPT | High — established case law | Growing |
| Directed trust statutes | Yes | Yes |

Nevada wins on the statute of limitations and the narrowest exception creditor carve-outs. A creditor in Nevada has 2 years to challenge a transfer into the trust (vs. 4 in Wyoming). After 2 years — the assets are essentially untouchable. For a spendthrift life insurance trust this is the correct jurisdiction.

**The trust is formed in Nevada even if you live in Texas (or any other state).** Nevada law governs the trust. A Nevada-licensed trustee (or trust company) may be required to satisfy the jurisdictional requirement — factor this into attorney selection.

## THE ILIT — WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT MUST DO

An **Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT)** is a specialized irrevocable trust whose primary asset is a life insurance policy. The trust owns the policy — not you personally. This accomplishes four things simultaneously:

1. **Estate tax elimination:** The policy death benefit passes outside your taxable estate (IRC §2042)
2. **Creditor protection:** Spendthrift clause makes the policy unreachable by personal creditors
3. **Tax architecture:** Non-grantor status + trust filing (Form 1041) separates trust income from personal income
4. **Control:** You are the trustee (or manage via a directed trust structure) — you control the asset without owning it

## THE TRUST INSTRUMENT — REQUIRED PROVISIONS

The attorney drafts the trust instrument. These provisions must be in it:

**1. IRREVOCABILITY CLAUSE**
The trust cannot be amended, modified, or revoked after execution. This is what gives it the legal protection. Without it — it's just a revocable trust with no asset protection.

```
Language: "This Trust is irrevocable and cannot be amended, modified, 
revoked, or terminated by the Grantor or any other person after execution."
```

**2. NON-GRANTOR STATUS**
The trust must be structured as a non-grantor trust for the K-1 architecture to work. This means the trust is a separate taxpayer — files its own Form 1041 rather than flowing income back to your personal 1040.

Key structural element: the Grantor must not retain enough control to trigger grantor trust status under IRC §§671-679. The attorney achieves this by limiting the Grantor's retained powers.

**3. SPENDTHRIFT CLAUSE**
```
Language: "No beneficiary of this Trust shall have the right to 
alienate, assign, transfer, or encumber any interest in the Trust, 
nor shall any interest of any beneficiary be subject to the claims 
of creditors or liable for any debts, obligations, or liabilities 
of any beneficiary."
```
This is the core creditor-protection provision. It prevents any creditor from attaching to the trust's assets (including the life insurance policy and its CSV).

**4. CRUMMEY POWERS**
This is required for premium payments to be gift-tax eligible under the annual exclusion.

When you (or the LLC via split-dollar) make a contribution to the ILIT to pay premiums, it is technically a gift to the trust's beneficiaries. To qualify for the annual gift tax exclusion ($18,000/person in 2024), the gift must be a "present interest" — meaning the beneficiary must have an immediate right to take the money.

Crummey powers give beneficiaries a **30-day withdrawal window** on each contribution. They almost never exercise it (and the attorney will structure the trust to make exercise undesirable). But the right existing is what makes the gift a "present interest" and qualifies it for the exclusion.

```
Language: "Within thirty (30) days of receiving written notice 
of a contribution to the Trust, each current beneficiary shall 
have the right to withdraw their proportionate share of such 
contribution. This withdrawal right shall lapse at the end of 
the thirty (30) day period."
```

**NOTICE REQUIREMENT:** You must send written Crummey notices to beneficiaries each time a contribution is made. Attorney will provide template. This is administrative but mandatory.

**5. POLICY OWNERSHIP AUTHORITY**
The trust instrument must explicitly grant the trustee authority to:
- Own, apply for, and hold life insurance policies
- Exercise all ownership rights (borrow against CSV, surrender, assign)
- Request policy loans from the insurer
- Use policy loan proceeds for trust investment purposes
- Enter into loan agreements with related entities (the LLC)

```
Language: "The Trustee is authorized and directed to own, acquire, 
hold, apply for, and maintain life insurance policies... and to 
exercise all incidents of ownership including the right to borrow 
against the cash surrender value of any policy..."
```

**6. INVESTMENT AUTHORITY + LLC LENDING AUTHORITY**
```
Language: "The Trustee may, in its discretion, make loans to 
any entity in which a beneficiary has an interest, provided 
such loans are made on commercially reasonable terms at or above 
the applicable federal rate as published by the Internal Revenue 
Service."
```
This provision authorizes the ILIT → LLC loan mechanism. Without it, the trustee may not have legal authority to make the loan.

**7. TRUSTEE SUCCESSION**
Who is the trustee? Three options:
- **You as trustee:** Simplest, most control, but may reduce asset protection in some creditor scenarios (grantor trust risk if too much retained control)
- **Directed trust structure:** You are the "investment trustee" / "distribution adviser," a Nevada trust company is the "administrative trustee." Nevada law allows this bifurcation. Best of both worlds.
- **Third-party trustee:** Maximum protection but least control.

**Recommended:** Directed trust structure with a Nevada trust company as administrative trustee. You retain de facto control over investment decisions without legally holding ownership. This is the sovereign architecture — control without ownership.

**8. BENEFICIARY DESIGNATION**
Name your beneficiaries (family members, future children, charitable foundation). The trust can include dynasty provisions allowing the trust to continue indefinitely for multiple generations.

**9. NEVADA GOVERNING LAW CLAUSE**
```
Language: "This Trust Agreement shall be governed by and construed 
in accordance with the laws of the State of Nevada, without regard 
to conflicts of laws principles."
```

## THE SEQUENCE — ORDER OF OPERATIONS IS CRITICAL

```
STEP 1: Attorney drafts trust instrument in Nevada
STEP 2: Trust instrument executed (signed, notarized)
STEP 3: Trust obtains its own EIN from IRS (separate from your SSN and LLC EIN)
STEP 4: Trust opens its own bank account (trust EIN, Nevada address)
STEP 5: Insurance agent opens WHOLE LIFE POLICY in the TRUST'S NAME
         — policy owner = ILIT
         — insured = Morph (you)
         — beneficiary = ILIT (trust itself, then distributes per terms)
STEP 6: Split-dollar agreement signed BEFORE first premium payment
STEP 7: LLC loans funds to ILIT for premium payment via split-dollar
STEP 8: Crummey notices sent to beneficiaries within 30 days of each contribution
```

**The order is non-negotiable.** If the policy is issued in your personal name first and then "transferred" to the ILIT — the 3-year clawback rule applies (IRC §2035). The death benefit stays in your estate for 3 years after transfer. The policy must originate in the ILIT.

## PROMISSORY NOTE ARCHITECTURE (ILIT → LLC)

Every time the ILIT loans money to the LLC, a promissory note is required. This is not optional — without it the IRS recharacterizes the transaction as a distribution or gift.

**Promissory Note Template Structure:**

```
PROMISSORY NOTE

Date:           [date of wire transfer]
Principal:      $[amount]
Lender:         [ILIT Name], a Nevada irrevocable trust (EIN: [trust EIN])
Borrower:       [LLC Name], a [state] limited liability company (EIN: [LLC EIN])
Interest Rate:  [current AFR for term length]% per annum
Term:           [short/mid/long-term based on loan duration]
Repayment:      [annual interest minimum; principal at maturity OR accelerated prepayment allowed]
Purpose:        [Specific: "STIS trading account capitalization" / "business investment"]
Security:       Unsecured [or describe if secured]

Trustee Signature:   _________________________ Date: _______
LLC Manager Signature: ______________________ Date: _______
```

**AFR lookup:** IRS publishes the Applicable Federal Rate monthly at irs.gov/rulings/applicable-federal-rates. Pull the rate for the month the note is executed. Short-term = loans under 3 years. Mid-term = 3-9 years. Long-term = over 9 years.

Using a rate BELOW the AFR = imputed income to the ILIT (IRS taxes the difference). Using the AFR or above = clean transaction.

**Repayment records:** Keep a simple ledger. Each time LLC sends a payment to the ILIT — record it with date, amount, principal vs. interest breakdown. CPA will need this for the 1041.

**Why not IUL?**
IUL (Indexed Universal Life) ties CSV growth to a market index with participation caps and floors. It sounds like more upside. It's not. The upside is capped (usually 10-12%), the internal cost-of-insurance charges escalate with age and eat returns in later years, and CSV is NOT guaranteed. Whole life CSV is contractually guaranteed. For a private banking function you need guaranteed capital — not indexed speculation. Use the STIS and investment accounts for market exposure. The policy is your bank, not your brokerage.

**Why not term?**
Term has no cash value. No policy loan capability. No banking function. It is insurance only. Completely wrong instrument for this architecture.

## NON-DIRECT RECOGNITION — THE CRITICAL CRITERION

In most whole life policies, when you take a policy loan, the loan balance is subtracted from the CSV before the dividend is calculated. This means you're paying loan interest AND losing dividend credit on the borrowed amount. The double compounding breaks.

**Non-direct recognition** = the insurer calculates your dividend as if no loan exists. You pay the loan interest, but your full CSV still earns the full dividend simultaneously. This is the double compounding.

Example at $100K CSV with 5% dividend rate:
- Direct recognition + $80K loan = dividend calculated on $20K = $1,000 dividend
- Non-direct recognition + $80K loan = dividend calculated on $100K = $5,000 dividend

The difference is $4,000 per year. On a $500K policy — $20,000/year. This is not a preference. It is a requirement.

## READING THE ILLUSTRATION

When the illustration comes back, check these numbers:

**Year 3 CSV test:**
- Total premiums paid by Year 3 = X
- CSV at Year 3 = should be 70-85% of X in a well-designed policy
- If CSV at Year 3 is below 50% of premiums paid = poorly designed (too much death benefit, not enough PUA)

**Year 5 CSV test:**
- CSV at Year 5 should approach or exceed total premiums paid (break-even)
- Standard whole life without IBC design: break-even is Year 10-12
- Well-designed IBC policy: break-even is Year 5-7
- This is the clearest signal of design quality

**Year 10 CSV test:**
- CSV at Year 10 should significantly exceed total premiums paid
- Target: $1.20-$1.40 for every $1.00 paid in, in the guaranteed column
- Non-guaranteed (dividend) column should show further upside

**PUA ratio:**
- Ask the agent: "What percentage of my total annual premium is going to the PUA rider vs. the base policy?"
- Target: 60-75% of total annual premium as PUAs
- If the agent doesn't know or says "it doesn't matter" — end the conversation

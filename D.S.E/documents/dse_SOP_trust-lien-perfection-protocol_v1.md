## WHAT THIS IS

Each trust already has the legal authority to hold title, lend against, and take a security interest in property (Article VI, §6.5–6.7 of each trust document — already drafted, already signed). This SOP makes that authority procedural: how the trust actually places and perfects a lien on property owned by the LLCs underneath it, so that before any creditor, judgment, or claim against an LLC can reach an asset, the trust's interest stands first in line.

This is standard secured-transactions practice under UCC Article 9 — not a novel theory. The "lien on everything" framing from the source video is real for property; it is not real, and is explicitly excluded, for people.

## PHASE 1 — ESTABLISH THE SECURED PARTY RELATIONSHIP

**Gate:** Trust EIN + LLC EIN both exist. LLC has acquired or is acquiring titled property.

1. **Promissory note or capital contribution agreement** between the trust and the LLC documenting that the trust is the source of funds for the property (whether by loan or capital contribution at formation).
   - If structured as a loan: must bear interest at no less than AFR (same requirement already in each trust's Article V Loan Authority), written repayment schedule, signed by both Trustee and LLC Manager.
   - If structured as capital contribution: trust receives a membership interest or preferred return reflected in the LLC operating agreement.

2. **Security agreement** between the trust (Secured Party) and the LLC (Debtor) — describes the collateral (the specific vehicle, property, or equipment) and grants the trust a security interest in it.

---

## PHASE 2 — PERFECT THE LIEN

Perfection is what makes the lien enforceable against third parties (other creditors, a court, a buyer) — an unperfected security interest is just a private agreement nobody else has to respect.

3. **Vehicles:** File the lien with the state DMV/title office in the state where the LLC is registered (Wyoming). The trust is listed as lienholder on the title. Title must show the lien before any sale or transfer can clear.

4. **Real property:** Record the security instrument (deed of trust or mortgage naming the trust as beneficiary) with the county recorder where the property sits.

5. **Equipment / other personal property:** File a UCC-1 Financing Statement with the Wyoming Secretary of State, naming the trust as Secured Party and the LLC as Debtor, describing the collateral. This is a standard commercial filing — same form businesses use for equipment financing, not a personal sovereignty filing.

---

## PHASE 3 — MAINTAIN PRIORITY

6. **Continuation statements:** UCC-1 filings lapse after 5 years in Wyoming. Calendar a reminder to file a continuation statement before expiration to keep priority intact.

7. **Record-keeping:** Keep the promissory note/security agreement, the filed UCC-1 (or title/deed showing the lien), and any continuation statements together in the trust's permanent records — same location as the trust document itself.

---

## WHY THIS MATTERS (the legitimate version of "lien on everything")

Once perfected, the trust's security interest has priority over later creditors of the LLC. If the LLC is ever sued or a creditor seeks to attach LLC assets, the trust's claim — properly filed and dated — gets paid first, ahead of the unsecured creditor. This is exactly the protective effect the source video was describing, achieved through a real, recognized commercial filing instead of an invented one.

This does **not** extend to, and was never legally capable of extending to, a person. A lien is a claim on property in commerce. A child, spouse, or yourself is not collateral, and no filing changes that.

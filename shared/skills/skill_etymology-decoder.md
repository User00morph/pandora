## WHAT THIS SKILL IS

A reusable process for decoding the original/legal/root meaning of a term against its current colloquial meaning — surfacing where statutory or commercial redefinition has shifted a word's operative meaning away from common usage. This is the same move Don Killam, sovereign-entity research, and Kemetic-root research all make independently: the gap between root meaning and current usage is where leverage or distortion lives.

---

## WHEN TO TRIGGER

```
ACTIVATE WHEN:
  - A legal/statutory term's definition is being verified against a claim (e.g. "person," "resident," "vehicle," "citizen")
  - A Kemetic, Hebrew, Greek, or Latin root is being traced for a name, title, or doctrine term
  - A research source makes a claim that hinges on "what this word ACTUALLY means"
  - Cross-referencing Black's Law Dictionary or another authoritative term source against a decode
```

## THE PROCESS — FOUR STEPS

### STEP 1 — IDENTIFY THE CLAIM
Isolate the exact term and the exact claim being made about it. ("Don Killam claims 'United States' is statutorily defined as a federal corporation under 28 U.S.C. §3002(15)(A).")

### STEP 2 — SOURCE THE DEFINITION
Pull the definition from the most authoritative tier available, in this order:
1. The actual statute/code text (Tier 1)
2. Black's Law Dictionary — **edition matters.** Use the edition appropriate to the claim:
   - **2nd edition (1910)** — public domain, freely available (archive.org, lawnix.com). Preferred for sovereign/common-law research specifically *because* it predates 20th-century statutory redefinitions that later editions reflect. If a claim depends on the "original" legal meaning of a term, the 2nd edition is the correct reference, not the latest.
   - **Latest edition (11th, 2019, Thomson Reuters)** — copyrighted, commercially licensed. Use only if Morph has acquired a legitimate copy (purchase, library access, institutional subscription). Do not source a pirated PDF — flag this constraint to Morph rather than silently working around it.
3. Root-language dictionaries (Lewis & Short Latin, Strong's Hebrew/Greek, Wallis Budge Egyptian, etc.) for non-legal etymology
4. General etymology references (etymonline.com) for common-usage drift

### STEP 3 — COMPARE: ROOT/STATUTORY MEANING vs. CURRENT COLLOQUIAL MEANING
Lay out explicitly:
- What the term meant at its root or in its original statutory context
- What the term means in current common usage
- Where and why the gap opened (redefinition within a specific act, semantic drift, deliberate obscuring)

### STEP 4 — TIER THE FINDING
Apply standard D.R.D source tiering to the conclusion:
- Tier 1: the statute/dictionary entry itself, verified
- Tier 3: the interpretation of what that definitional gap means practically
- Flag explicitly when a claim conflates "the definition exists" (often Tier 1) with "therefore X follows" (often Tier 3-4)

## BLACK'S LAW DICTIONARY — ACQUISITION NOTE

The latest edition is commercially copyrighted (Thomson Reuters) — do not source a pirated copy. Two paths:
1. Morph acquires a legitimate copy (digital or physical) and files it as raw research material per the Data Refinement Law — it enters `D.R.D/research/` as raw until cited/refined.
2. For research specifically about *original* legal meaning (the use case most of Pandora's sovereign-entity research actually needs), the public-domain 2nd edition (1910) is usually the more correct reference anyway, not a workaround.
